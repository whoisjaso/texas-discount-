# Texas dealer fees: the rulebook

**As of 2026-10-03.** Every figure below was read from a primary source on or
just before that date and then checked by two independent fact-checkers: one
for statutes and rules, one for agency guidance. Re-check this file before
every new dealer build and every January (section 6). If the law changes,
update this file first and the desk second, and keep both in step.

This file is the single legal reference for what a Texas dealer may charge on
top of the vehicle price. The desk's fee module mirrors the machine-readable
block in section 5.10 and must not carry a figure that is not in this file.

**Status labels used below**

- **Confirmed**: primary text read and confirmed by both checkers.
- **Corrected**: the first research had a different value. The corrected
  value is the one used here (section 7 lists every correction).
- **Agency guidance**: no statute or rule says it, but an agency
  publication does. Treated as binding in practice.
- **Unverified**: no primary source found. The safer handling is stated
  beside it.
- **Platform policy**: the desk's own safer choice where the law is silent.
  It is not presented as law.

---

## The answer, in plain words

1. **Texas has no single combined cap on dealer fees**, for franchised or
   independent dealers. No statute or rule sets a combined total (section 4).
2. **The number the user remembered is $225.** It is the **documentary
   fee** limit: a doc fee of $225.00 or less is *presumed reasonable*
   (7 TAC §84.205(b)(1), effective 2024-07-11). A dealer may charge more only
   after filing a notice and a cost analysis with the OCCC (Office of
   Consumer Credit Commissioner), and never more than it filed. It is a
   presumption, not a hard statutory ceiling, but going over it without a
   filing exposes the dealer to restitution orders.
3. **The $225 is the same for franchised and independent dealers.** The real
   split is by vehicle type: motorcycles, mopeds, ATVs and towable RVs
   financed under Finance Code ch. 345 have a hard maximum of $200.
4. **Any charge for handling or processing paperwork counts as the doc fee,
   whatever it is called** ("admin", "processing", "e-file", "paperwork",
   "title service", "compliance"). It all adds into one total, and that
   total is what the $225 limits.
5. **Only two other dealer charges may sit on top of the price**, and only
   when they apply:
   - a dealer-deputy title fee of up to $10, only if the county has
     deputized the dealer;
   - a vehicle inventory tax (VIT) reimbursement, by formula, only for an
     eligible dealer.
6. **Everything else the dealer charges goes inside the vehicle price**,
   where it is taxed and advertised: prep, reconditioning, "dealer
   service", add-ons.
7. **Government fees (title, registration, plate, inspection) are passed
   through at the amount actually paid.** They do not count toward the
   $225, and the dealer cannot mark them up.

So the desk's ceiling on dealer charges on top of the price is:

**$225 (or the OCCC-filed maximum) + $10 if deputized + VIT if eligible.**
For a dealer with nothing else recorded, that is **$225.00**.

### 1.2 The doc fee notice: exact text and placement

**English (Ch. 348), word for word, comma and last sentence included:**

> A DOCUMENTARY FEE IS NOT AN OFFICIAL FEE. A DOCUMENTARY FEE IS NOT REQUIRED BY LAW, BUT MAY BE CHARGED TO BUYERS FOR HANDLING DOCUMENTS RELATING TO THE SALE. A DOCUMENTARY FEE MAY NOT EXCEED A REASONABLE AMOUNT AGREED TO BY THE PARTIES. THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY LAW.

Source: Tex. Fin. Code §348.006(c)(3)(B). Wording from HB 3621 (eff.
2009-09-01); section last amended 2017-09-01. Confirmed.

**Spanish: the two OCCC-approved translations** (OCCC Advisory Bulletin
B09-3, 2009-07-02, still current). Use one of them verbatim and never a fresh
translation. Pick one option and use it on every Spanish document.

> Option 1: UN HONORARIO DE DOCUMENTACIÓN NO ES UN HONORARIO OFICIAL. UN HONORARIO DE DOCUMENTACIÓN NO ES REQUERIDO POR LA LEY, PERO PUEDE SER CARGADA AL COMPRADOR COMO GASTOS DE MANEJO DE DOCUMENTOS RELACIONADOS CON UNA VENTA. UN HONORARIO DE DOCUMENTACIÓN NO PUEDE EXCEDER UNA CANTIDAD RAZONABLE ACORDADA POR LAS PARTES. ESTA NOTIFICACIÓN ES REQUERIDA POR LA LEY.

> Option 2: UN CARGO DOCUMENTAL NO ES UN CARGO OFICIAL. LA LEY NO EXIGE QUE SE IMPONGA UN CARGO DOCUMENTAL. PERO ÉSTE PODRÍA COBRARSE A LOS COMPRADORES POR EL MANEJO DE LA DOCUMENTACIÓN EN RELACIÓN CON LA VENTA. UN CARGO DOCUMENTAL NO PUEDE EXCEDER UNA CANTIDAD RAZONABLE ACORDADA POR LAS PARTES. ESTA NOTIFICACIÓN SE EXIGE POR LEY.

**Ch. 345 vehicles (motorcycles, ATVs and the like) use different wording**
(Fin. Code §345.251(c)(2)):

> A DOCUMENTARY FEE IS NOT AN OFFICIAL FEE. A DOCUMENTARY FEE IS NOT REQUIRED BY LAW, BUT MAY BE CHARGED TO BUYERS FOR HANDLING DOCUMENTS RELATING TO THE SALE. A DOCUMENTARY FEE MAY NOT EXCEED A REASONABLE AMOUNT AGREED TO BY THE PARTIES THAT IS NOT MORE THAN THE MAXIMUM AMOUNT ALLOWED BY THE STATE. THIS NOTICE IS REQUIRED BY LAW.

Under Ch. 345, the notice must also appear on any preliminary worksheet that
computes the sale price and is shown to the buyer (§345.251(c)).

**Where the notice goes**

- **On paper.** On **both** the buyer's order (the desk's bill of sale) and
  the retail installment contract, in reasonable proximity to the doc fee
  amount, in type that is bold-faced, capitalized, underlined or otherwise
  conspicuous (§348.006(c)(3)). **Platform policy:** print it on every bill
  of sale, cash sales included.
- **Posted.** Clearly visible in each place where a sale is finalized
  (§348.006(d)). **Platform policy (an interpretation, not settled law):**
  also show it on the remote e-sign screen next to the doc fee.
- **In the buyer's language.** If the oral sales presentation was in a
  language other than the contract's, give the buyer a written copy in the
  presentation language (§348.006(d)). On the desk, a Spanish-language deal
  whose document is in English prints the approved Spanish notice beside the
  English one.

### 1.3 Every other charge the dealer imposes

- **The closed list.** On a retail installment contract, only these may sit
  outside the cash price:
  - registration, title and license fees, plus deputy fees authorized
    under Transp. Code §520.0071;
  - taxes (the VIT counts as a tax, per OCCC Interpretation 94-1);
  - fees prescribed by law for the sale or inspection;
  - insurance, service contracts, warranties, auto club memberships,
    trade-in credit agreements and debt cancellation agreements;
  - the documentary fee.

  Sources: Fin. Code §348.005 (as amended eff. 2017-09-01) and
  §348.006(a). Confirmed. The OCCC's orders say: "Chapter 348 does not
  authorize any other itemized charges to be included in the principal
  balance of a retail installment contract."
- **OCCC enforcement examples.** All are Agreed Orders. In each one the
  dealer had to move dealer-service charges into the cash price and into
  any advertised price.

  | Order | Dealer | Signed | Charge | Penalty |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | L24-00112 | Gunn Acura, franchised | 2024-09-26 | $250 "DEALER SERVICE FEE" itemized, about 800 transactions | $20,000 |
  | L24-00113 | Gunn Buick-GMC, sister store | 2024-09-26 | 176 transactions with improperly disclosed dealer service fees; no amount stated | $4,000 |
  | L25-035 | HiLine Motors, independent | 2024-11-12 | $299 "Prep Service Fees" and a $698 "Service Fee" | $8,970 |
  | L25-038 | Patterson Motors of Kilgore | 2025-01-03 | $298 "dealer prep & tint" | $46,275 |

  The first research attributed the $250 fee to Gunn Buick-GMC
  (L24-00113). That is **corrected**: it was Gunn Acura (L24-00112).
- **Any charge for handling or processing documents is the doc fee**,
  whatever its label. Fin. Code §348.006(a)(1)(C) defines the doc fee as a
  fee "for services rendered for or on behalf of the retail buyer in
  handling and processing documents relating to the motor vehicle sale".
  It counts toward the $225 (or the filed amount). Confirmed.
- **Prep, reconditioning, detail, installed tint or etch, "dealer service",
  markup:**
  - no dollar cap;
  - must be **inside the cash price** (§348.004(c));
  - must be in any advertised price (43 TAC §215.250(a): "Destination and
    dealer preparation charges and additional dealership markup, if any
    must be included in the featured sales price");
  - taxed as part of the price (Comptroller 96-254). This is agency
    guidance: Tax Code §152.002(b)(3) excludes labor "in installing,
    applying, remodeling, or repairing the motor vehicle sold", so the
    statute sits in some tension with taxing pre-sale prep. Follow the
    Comptroller.
- **Cash sales.** Ch. 348 does not govern a pure cash sale, but the
  advertising rule and the FTC Act still apply. **Platform policy:** the
  same structure for cash and credit.
- **Optional products are never required.** GAP and debt cancellation
  (§348.124), service contracts (Occ. Code §1304.161(b)), trade-in credit
  (§348.125(d)), auto club (§348.414(b)), and protection products not
  installed at sale (§348.014; requiring them is a deceptive trade
  practice). Confirmed. Their limits are in section 3.

### 1.5 Plate database compliance fee (Transp. Code §503.0631(f))

The statute still reads: "The dealer may charge a reasonable fee not to exceed
$20 for costs associated with complying with this section." Current TxDMV
guidance says the opposite:

- Dealer Manual 2026, p. 135: "Dealers may not charge any other amount for
  the plates."
- HB 718 Dealers' FAQ (2025-03-21): "You cannot charge more than $10."

The sources conflict, so the safer, lower limit applies. **Platform: $0
(off).** If the dealer's counsel approves it, it is a document-handling
charge and counts inside the doc fee total, so it never adds on top of the
$225. Status: confirmed conflict.

## 2. State-set fees: the dealer only passes these through

**Rules for every government line**

- **The amount on the line must equal the amount actually paid** to the
  public official, and the dealer keeps the receipt. The receipt is the
  TxDMV Title Application Receipt (VTR-500-RTS), or an inspection receipt
  for an itemized inspection charge.
  - Sources: Fin. Code §348.005(1), (3); 7 TAC §84.707(d)(2)(A)(iv),
    §84.708(e)(2)(A)(v); OCCC Agreed Order L25-087, Bayside Mitsubishi
    (2025-08-20): "cease and desist charging title and registration fees in
    amounts that are not actually paid to public officials."
  - These amounts never count toward the doc fee cap, and are never marked
    up.
- **webDEALER computes them.** Every dealer must file the title and
  registration application in the buyer's name through webDEALER (Transp.
  Code §520.0055, mandatory since 2025-07-01).
  - The dealer must file within 30 days, or 45 days on a seller-financed
    sale (Transp. Code §501.145; Tax Code §152.069(a)).
  - "Each duty imposed by this section on the seller of a motor vehicle is
    solely that of the seller" (§501.0234(c)).
  - "Giving the Title Work to an In-state Consumer is Prohibited" (Dealer
    Manual 2026 §4(n)).
  - Where the desk's computed figure and webDEALER's computed figure
    differ, **webDEALER's figure wins.**
- **Same for franchised and independent dealers.** Differences come only
  from selling **new** vehicles (section 2.4).

  for each further 30 days, up to $250.)

  - dealer temporary plate: $10 each (§503.008(a)(2));

    motorcycles, $100-$500 for trailers and motor homes. Charged only when

  - Late tax payment penalty: 5% if 1-30 days late, 10% after 30 days,

## 4. Is there one combined cap on dealer fees?

**No.** No combined total on dealer fees appears anywhere in the Texas
statutes or rules read on 2026-10-03, for franchised or independent dealers.

- **Statutes read:** Fin. Code ch. 303, 342, 345, 348, 354; Transp. Code
  ch. 501, 502, 503, 520, 548; Tax Code ch. 23, 152; Occ. Code ch. 1304,
  2301; Bus. & Com. Code ch. 3, 17; Health & Safety Code ch. 386.
- **Rules read:** 7 TAC §§84.201-.205, 84.308-.309, 84.501, 84.604,
  84.707-.708, 86.201; 43 TAC §§215.155, 215.242, 215.250, 217.162-.168,
  217.183, 217.185; 30 TAC §§114.2, 114.50, 114.53, 114.80, 114.87.
- The term "documentary fee" appears only in Fin. Code ch. 345 and ch. 348.

This conclusion comes from the absence of any such provision; no source
states it outright.

**What works like a combined cap:** the closed list in §348.005 plus the doc
fee rule. Together they mean only three dealer-kept charges can ever sit on
top of the price:

```
dealer charges on top of the price
    =  documentary-fee bucket   <= $225.00, or the OCCC-filed maximum
                                   (every document/processing charge, any label)
    +  dealer-deputy title fee  <= $10.00, only if county-deputized,
                                   at the county-approved amount
    +  VIT reimbursement        <= net sales price x unit property tax factor,
                                   only if in business on Jan 1 and an eligible sale
```

With nothing else recorded, that is **$225.00**. Everything else the dealer
charges belongs in the (taxed) vehicle price. Government fees pass through
at cost, outside this total.

## 5. How the desk enforces it

The goal: **no matter how the dealer structures its fees, the total of
dealer-kept charges on top of the price can never exceed the limit**, and
no government line can carry dealer margin.

### 5.1 Principles

1. **One source of numbers.** One pure module holds every figure in section
   5.10, each with its citation and as-of date. Nothing else in the desk
   types a fee amount. The "no hardcoded dealer facts" guard still applies:
   the dealer's own figures (doc fee, deputy fee, VIT factor) are facts the
   owner supplies, never code.
2. **Dealer-set vs state-set.**
   - The owner sets only the dealer lines: the doc fee, the deputy fee if
     deputized, and VIT if eligible.
   - Government lines are computed from the buyer's county and the vehicle,
     shown read-only with their source, and confirmed against webDEALER.
     The owner never types a registration fee.
3. **The bucket.** Every dealer charge for paperwork, whatever the dealer
   calls it, adds into the doc fee bucket, and the bucket is capped. There
   is no free-form dealer fee line anywhere in the desk.
4. **Refuse, don't trim.** An over-limit figure is refused with the reason
   and the citation. The desk never silently lowers it, and it never
   loosens an existing filing refusal.
5. **Uniform.**
   - One doc fee per location, the same for every buyer, cash or credit.
   - It is not editable on a deal. A discount goes into the vehicle price.
   - A filed bill of sale keeps the fee it was filed with (the SOP Freeze).
     A new fee applies only to bills of sale filed after it was saved.

### 5.2 Owner onboarding: the Dealer Fees step

The desk's specification of this step, with everything else onboarding
includes, is the SOP's "First sign-in: onboarding" (item 3, built as one
unit at premium-dealer-build step 4.3); this section is the law behind it.

Fees are dealership facts, not personal ones. The step therefore appears in
the first sign-in corridor **only for the Owner role**:

Choose A Password → What Is Your Name? → Draw Your Signature → **Dealer
Fees** → Done.

- **Who and when.**
  - It is shown to an Owner while the dealership's fees are unset.
  - It is skipped for every other role, and for an Owner once the fees are
    set.
  - Later changes are made by an owner on the account pages, and every
    change is logged.
- **Same treatment as the corridor's other screens.** One question per
  screen, English and Spanish, and refusals come from the message
  catalogue. Each screen shows the citation in one quiet line.

Screens, in order:

1. **Do You Write Finance Contracts?** (buy here pay here, or contracts
   assigned to a lender)
   - Yes asks for the OCCC license numbers: NMLS ID plus the legacy ALECS
     license number (the license moved to NMLS in 2026).
   - The answer decides whether the dealer is a Ch. 348 "retail seller".
     The $225 applies either way, by platform policy.
2. **What Is Your Documentary Fee?** One dollar figure for this location,
   charged to every buyer.
   - Shown beside it: "Up to $225 is presumed reasonable (7 TAC
     §84.205)."
   - **Refused above $225.00** unless the owner taps "I filed a higher
     maximum with the OCCC". That reveals the filed maximum, the filing
     date, the date it took effect, the license or NMLS number, and the
     location. Every field is required.
   - The fee is then refused above the filed maximum.
   - A dealership that changed owners in the last 30 days is warned about
     §84.205(c)(4).
   - $0 is a valid answer.
3. **Are You A Dealer Deputy?** (deputized by your county tax office to
   process titles)
   - Yes asks for the county-approved title convenience fee, **refused
     above $10.00**.
   - No sets it to $0 and hides it on every deal.
4. **Vehicle Inventory Tax.**
   - Asks whether the dealer was in business on January 1 of this year.
   - If yes, asks whether it passes the VIT on to buyers, and for the unit
     property tax factor from its VIT statement.
   - Not in business on January 1 means VIT is $0, and the screen says
     why.
5. **Do You Finance Motorcycles, ATVs, Mopeds Or Towable RVs?**
   - Yes caps the doc fee on those Ch. 345 contracts at $200 ($250 when a
     contract covers both land vehicles and watercraft).
   - The desk does not write Ch. 345 contracts today. Until it does, a
     "yes" is recorded and reported, and nothing more is enabled.
6. **Review.**
   - Shows the government fees for the dealer's own county, read-only with
     sources: title, registration, local fee, P&H, inspection replacement,
     emissions, plate.
   - Shows the running line "Dealer charges on top of the price: $X of $Y
     allowed".
   - Saving writes the figures with the date, the member who saved them
     and the as-of date of this rulebook.

**Not asked:**

- a registration or title fee (those are the state's);
- an "other fees", admin, processing or e-file fee (that is the doc fee or
  the price);
- the plate-database fee (off; section 1.5).

### 5.3 On every deal

- **Government lines**
  - Computed from the buyer's county and the vehicle facts: the title fee
    by county of residence; base registration by weight and class;
    TexasSure; the local fee from a year-stamped Fee Chart 1C table; P&H;
    the inspection replacement fee on its own line; the $10 plate fee on
    every retail sale unless exempt; the EV fee.
  - The emissions fee is included only when the dealer and the buyer are
    both in emissions counties and the vehicle is subject to testing. It
    is taken from webDEALER, never hard-coded.
  - Staff may replace a computed line only with webDEALER's computed
    figure, recorded as such.
  - A county missing from the table, or a table older than the current
    year, refuses filing with "Confirm the fees in webDEALER".
- **Dealer lines**
  - The doc fee, the deputy fee and VIT, read from the onboarding record.
  - None is editable on the deal. VIT may be lowered or waived on the deal,
    never raised above the formula.
- **Tax base** = vehicle price (with any prep or add-ons inside it) −
  trade-in. The doc fee, VIT, deputy fee and government fees stay
  untaxed. This matches today's `calcTexasTax(price)`.
- **Out-the-door split.** The fixed amount taken out before the tax is
  backed out is the sum of every untaxed line: government lines, doc fee,
  deputy fee and VIT.
- **The reverse calculation is gone.** The SOP's "doc fee = total fees
  minus title minus registration, so it absorbs any statutory change" is
  **not allowed**:
  - a government change may never be absorbed by the doc fee;
  - the doc fee may never be backed out of a quoted total;
  - Triple J's $292 is above $225 and lawful only with an OCCC filing of at
    least $292.

### 5.4 Filing refusals to add

None replaces an existing refusal; each is an addition.

1. The doc fee is unset (exists today, stays).
2. The doc fee bucket is above $225.00 and no complete OCCC filing is
   recorded.
3. The doc fee bucket is above the recorded OCCC filed maximum.
4. Any dealer-imposed line outside {doc fee, deputy fee, VIT}. This
   includes the template path's free-form "Other Fees" step
   (`src/lib/documents/template-workflow.ts:265`). Offer instead: "Add it
   to the vehicle price (taxed)", or one of the lawful pass-through lines
   with a receipt: the emissions station fee, a full-service deputy fee
   webDEALER shows, the $0.50 webDEALER fee.
5. A deputy fee without deputy status, above $10.00, or above the
   county-approved amount.
6. VIT above (net price × factor), or above $0 when the dealer was not in
   business on January 1, or on a dealer, fleet or subsequent sale.
7. A government line that differs from the computed value without a
   recorded webDEALER figure.
8. A missing $10 plate fee on a non-exempt retail sale.
9. The inspection replacement fee folded into the registration line.
10. A bill of sale or contract template whose doc fee notice is not the
    exact statutory string (section 1.2): checked at build time by a test,
    and refused at filing if the template drifts.
11. Optional products:
    - GAP above 5% of the amount financed;
    - a trade-in credit agreement above 5% of the cash price;
    - any optional product without its own buyer opt-in;
    - a debt cancellation agreement without insurance, at all, until the
      §84.308(e) schedule is obtained.

### 5.6 Public site and advertising

Follow the FTC staff position (Sept 2026):

- The advertised price includes the doc fee and any mandatory dealer charge
  (VIT if always charged).
- Only taxes and the government title, registration, plate and inspection
  fees may be left out.
- The disclaimer then reads, in substance: "Price includes the $X
  documentary fee. Excludes tax, title, license and registration."
- Never "internet price", never a savings claim on a used car, and never a
  qualifier like "with trade" or "with dealer financing" (43 TAC
  §215.250(c)-(f)).

### 5.7 Tests to write

These are pure modules with no database. They add to the suite; none
replaces an existing test.

- **Doc fee cap**
  - $225.00 is accepted; $225.01 is refused without a filing.
  - With a recorded filing of $300.00: $300.00 is accepted and $300.01 is
    refused.
  - An incomplete filing record is refused.
- **Bucket.** Doc fee $200 + plate-database fee $30 (if ever enabled) is
  refused at $225.
- **No other lines.** Any nonzero dealer "other fee" refuses filing.
- **Deputy fee.** Refused without deputy status, above $10.00, and above
  the county-approved amount.
- **VIT.** Refused above the formula; refused when the dealer was not in
  business on January 1.
- **Government lines**
  - Harris 2026 gasoline sale = $33 + $51.75 + $11.50 + $4.75 + $7.50 +
    $2.75 + $10, with the $7.50 on its own line.
  - Title $28 for a non-affected, attainment county.
- **Tax** excludes the doc fee and VIT, and includes prep folded into the
  price.
- **Notice strings** equal the statute and B09-3 exactly. String
  equality, not "contains".
- **The SOP money vectors stay as they are.**
  - They test arithmetic with a $292 doc fee fixture
    (`vitest.config.ts`).
  - The legal cap is a separate validator applied at onboarding and at
    filing, not inside `saleMoney`.
  - Any test that **files** a document with the $292 fixture must also

## 6. Open questions and the re-check schedule

### 6.1 Questions for the owner (ask at onboarding; until answered, use the safer handling)

1. **Does the dealer write retail installment contracts** (buy here pay
   here) or assign them to lenders, and under which OCCC license (NMLS ID
   and legacy ALECS number)? Safer: apply $225 and the notice to every sale
   either way.
2. **Has the dealer filed a doc fee above $225 with the OCCC?** Record the
   filed maximum, the filing date, the date it took effect and the
   location. If it has not filed, the cap is $225. If its filing went
   through ALECS before the move to NMLS, confirm with the OCCC that it is
   still on file.
3. **Is the dealer a county-deputized dealer deputy, and at what approved
   fee?** If not, $0.
4. **VIT.** Was the dealer in business on January 1? What is its unit
   property tax factor, and does it pass VIT through? If unknown, $0.
5. **Does it finance Ch. 345 vehicles** (motorcycles, ATVs, mopeds, towable
   RVs)? If so, a $200 cap on those contracts, and the desk does not
   support them yet.
6. **The late-handling fee** on the Vehicle Responsibility form. Is there a
   legal basis for charging it to the buyer, given:
   - the filing duty is the dealer's alone (§501.0234(c));
   - the dealer owes its own $10 late fee (§501.146(a));
   - TxDMV prohibits giving title work to an in-state buyer (Dealer Manual
     §4(n))?

   Counsel should also review the form's premise that a buyer may "ask the
   dealer to complete the title transfer later". Until then the existing
   refusal stays.
7. **Did the dealership change hands in the last 30 days?** (§84.205(c)(4))

### 6.2 Points of law still unsettled (safer handling in force)

8. **Advertised price.** TxDMV allows excluding the doc fee; FTC staff
   (Sept 2026) require including it. The desk follows the FTC. Counsel
   should confirm before the site's disclaimers are changed.
9. **Emissions fee at registration.** The rule says $2.50; the counties
   publish $2.75. Use webDEALER's figure, and confirm Bexar's amount after
   2026-11-01.
10. **TexasSure: $1 or $2 on a dealer sale** (two $1 sentences in
    §502.357(a))? Use webDEALER's figure.
11. **The §503.0631(f) plate-database fee** ($20 in the statute, $0 in
    TxDMV guidance). Off unless counsel approves, and then inside the doc
    fee bucket.
12. **The full list of $33 title-fee counties.** The affected counties are
    listed above, but the federal nonattainment counties were not compiled
    from a primary source (Harris is confirmed). Until compiled, take the
    title fee from webDEALER or Form 130-U for any county outside the
    known lists.
13. **Does Harris County route webDEALER titles through a full-service
    deputy who adds up to $20?** Pass it through only when webDEALER shows
    it.
14. **Pre-sale prep and tax.** The Comptroller says it is taxable; Tax
    Code §152.002(b)(3) excludes installation and repair labor. Follow the
    Comptroller (prep goes in the taxed price).
15. **Debt cancellation agreements without insurance.** The 7 TAC
    §84.308(e) fee schedule was not retrieved. Product disabled.
16. **The finance contract form.** Is the desk's contract an OCCC model
    contract or a plain-language-approved form? The OCCC posted a Plain
    Language Rule Review draft on 2026-09-08. This affects whether contract
    wording (including the notice fix) can be edited freely.
17. **Not researched:**
    - Military Lending Act (36%) exposure on BHPH contracts sold to
      servicemembers;
    - out-of-state buyers (exemption forms, out-of-state plates, who files
      the title);
    - the authority behind the $0.50 webDEALER fee;
    - the Comptroller rule behind the certified appraisal fee range.
18. **Showing the posted notice on the e-sign screen** is an interpretation
    of §348.006(d), not something the OCCC has addressed.
